# Editorial Production Workflow

## States

IDEA → RESEARCHED → EDITORIAL_SELECTED → VISUAL_BRIEFED → RIGHTS_CLEARED → DE_RU_DRAFTED → REVIEWED → APPROVED → PUBLISHED → MEASURED

Do not label an item APPROVED until the owner approves it.

## Phase 1: Research

Verify event dates, collections, names and interesting facts from official fashion-week schedules, house websites or reliable fashion publications. Record access date and links. Distinguish Autumn/Winter 2026–2027 from Spring/Summer 2027 and from the calendar year of the runway event.

## Phase 2: Concepts

Generate 30 distinct stories, not a random batch of 30 photos. For each: title, subject, personal observation, visual motif, learning value and primary audience goal. Compare them in a table.

## Phase 3: Pilot selection

Select 10 concepts for an owner decision. Balance countries, stories, close-ups, full looks, accessories and colours.

## Phase 4: Bilingual copy & visual planning

Develop a native German and Russian caption, hook variants, question, keywords, restrained hashtags and accessibility text. Write a photographic brief covering framing, light, fabric, colours, crop and format.

## Phase 5: Source visuals

Check authenticity, copyright permissions, licensing and attribution conditions. If imagery is AI-generated, disclose it and do not present it as actual runway coverage. Do not start generating images until ideas are approved.

## Phase 6: Editorial review

Check: factual sources; licence; precise designer and season; natural German and Russian; no exaggerated claims; recognisable Helena voice; visual quality; approval status.

## Phase 7: Publish, learn, revise

Track two accounts separately and iterate based on results. Record changes in GitHub with descriptive commits.

## Definition of done

A story answers: why stop scrolling? what is learned or felt? why return for Helena's next observation?
